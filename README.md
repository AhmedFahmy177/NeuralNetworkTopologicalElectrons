# Deep Learning Electronic Topological Materials

## Project Overview:
- As the search for novel quantum materials continues to expand, the ability to accurately and efficiently identify topological phases has become increasingly crucial. Topological materials are central to cutting-edge technologies including fault-tolerant quantum computation, low-dissipation electronics, spin-momentum–locked transport, and emergent quantum information platforms. However, determining whether a material is topological typically requires resource-intensive techniques, such as angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES) or highly specialized density functional theory (DFT) calculations with spin-orbit coupling. These methods are either experimentally demanding or computationally expensive, making them impractical for large-scale screening across the tens of thousands of known crystalline compounds.

- This project aims to develop a deep-learning framework for predicting topological character directly from chemical composition, enabling rapid and scalable discovery of new candidate topological materials. Using a curated and labeled dataset of inorganic compounds, we train supervised learning models to classify materials whether they have topological electronic bands or not, where topology covers the different topological subclasses such as topological insulators (TI), topological crystalline insulators (TCI), high-symmetry line semimetals (HSLSM), and high-symmetry-point semimetals (HSPSM). Our strategy leverages two complementary data representations: composition-based vector encodings, which capture elemental contributions to topology, and graph- or attention-based models (e.g., Roost), which learn chemically informed interactions between constituent elements.
By replacing exhaustive DFT-based band topology calculations with fast, data-driven predictive models, this work enables high-throughput topological materials screening at negligible computational cost. Ultimately, such models provide physical insight into the elemental and symmetry-driven mechanisms underlying topological phases and support the targeted exploration of chemically and crystallographically plausible topological material candidates.

## Framework and Positioning of this project:
- In the last ~ 20 years, the development of the topological band theory in crystalline solids (for more technical details, refer to the review by Rev. Mod. Phys. 82, 3045 and references therein) has encountered shortage in the material realization of the different types of topology that had been theoretically studied. Partial solution to this problem came with the development of the symmetry-topology connection that has been formulated in what’s called ‘Topological Quantum Chemistry/ Symmetry Indicators’ theory (Nature 547, 298–305 (2017) & Nat Commun 8, 50 (2017)). According to this framework, topology can be inferred from the symmetry eigenvalues, in some symmetry settings. By a symmetry setting of a given material, we mean the Space Group (SG), i.e. the collection of the symmetries in a material. This has been employed effectively in working out high-throughput searches for topological materials (Nature 566, 475–479 (2019)), resulting in over 8,000 topological materials.
- However, in some symmetry settings (what we call the ‘discovery space’), symmetry alone is insufficient. Therefore, recent works (Topogivity 1st paper:  Nano Lett. 2023, 23, 3, 772–778  & Topogivity 2nd paper: Phys. Rev. B 109, 035122) tried to train symmetry-blind ML models over the dataset where topology was successfully diagnosed using symmetry (i.e. the Labelled Dataset) and applied them to the Discovery Dataset to see if the models can be successful in predicting new materials. These predictions are finally checked by DFT calculations.
- Topogivity 1st paper (idea is described in the video presentation) reported a model with 82.7% accuracy over the test set, with 68 new predicted materials. Among them, 56 were confirmed with DFT. Topogivity second paper built on the method of the 1st paper with a more complex rule than the 1st  paper where the topogivity factors are to be multiplied with a function of the fractional compositions that’s to be learned with a CNN instead of simple fractional composition linear factor in the 1st paper. This leveraged the accuracy up to 83.9%. They also extended to the case of magnetic materials where strong interactions play a significant role.
- Here, we build over the Topogivity 1st paper mainly utilizing the ElemNet model suggested by (Scientific Reports volume 8, Article number: 17593 (2018)) to relax the linearity condition adopted in the topogivity papers and allow for a more generic nonlinear rule (i.e. a NN) to see what maximum accuracy a NN can reach + predict novel topological materials, solely from the elemental composition. In the last part of this project, we briefly check the message-passing NN model, so called ROOST model, developed in (Nature Communications volume 11, Article number: 6280 (2020)) to see if the extra attention layers would enhance the model performance.

## KPIs: 
- Accuracy: Improvement in predicted classification of topological materials compared to a baseline classifier (i.e., dummy classifier with a stratified strategy) and compared to recent research papers (Nano Lett. 2023, 23, 3, 772–778 & Phys. Rev. B 109, 035122).
- Throughput: Prediction of novel topological electronic compounds, and efficient reduction of computational time compared to time-consuming DFT calculations.
## Approach:
The first part of the work focused on developing neural network classification models to predict whether a material is topological or trivial based on its chemical composition, using the Materiae database, which provides topological labels derived from first-principles calculations with spin–orbit coupling. The second part expanded this task to novel previously unexplored topological materials, enabling large-scale inference of topological behavior without performing computationally expensive band structure topology calculations.

- Feature Engineering: After excluding trivial metals and magnetic compounds which lie beyond the scope of this study, our dataset intentionally uses composition-only features, reflecting the principle that the chemical formula alone contains sufficient information to infer trends in band topology. Rather than relying on structural, thermodynamic, or electronic descriptors—which are either expensive to compute or unavailable for large materials spaces—we represent each compound through a composition vector, encoding the fractional presence of each element in the periodic table. This approach avoids the risk of introducing spurious identifiers or high-cardinality categorical features, and ensures that the learned model generalizes to new, previously unseen compositions. No handcrafted domain-specific features are used; the deep learning models are tasked with discovering chemically driven patterns in elemental contributions to topology directly from the data. Only the space group of each material is checked to assign it in either the labelled dataset (where symmetry can diagnose topology) or in the discovery dataset (where topology is not diagnosable by symmetries), as described in the slides.

- Training & Validation & Testing: For the binary topological classification task, we trained composition-based neural network models, including an ElemNet-style fully connected network and an attention-based Roost (MPNN) architecture, both operating directly on the elemental composition of each material. 70% of the dataset was used for training, with 5-fold cross validation adapted, while the remaining 30% was split evenly into validation and test sets. Model performance was evaluated using accuracy and macro-averaged F1 score, where accuracy captures overall correctness and macro-F1 ensures balanced evaluation despite class imbalance between trivial and topological materials.
For the Roost (MPNN) model, we represented each material by its elemental composition and allowed the network to learn chemically meaningful interactions through multi-head self-attention layers. Unlike the fully connected ElemNet architecture, Roost incorporates attention-based pooling to capture the contributions and co-dependencies of constituent elements in a composition. The model was trained using the same 70/15/15 train–validation–test split used for ElemNet, with binary cross-entropy loss for the two-class task. Training also employed early stopping based on validation macro-F1 score, ensuring that performance remained balanced even under class imbalance. Hyperparameters such as embedding dimension, number of attention layers, number of heads, hidden layer size, dropout rate, and learning rate were tuned using Optuna. After training, the held-out test set was used strictly as a final unbiased evaluation to assess the generalization performance of the best Roost configuration.

## Results:  
-	Outperformed recent literature models, mentioned above, with ~ 90.0% accuracy compared to the ~ 83.5% in literature, pushing the conjectured limit of chemical composition-only accurate prediction of topology, first argued in the first reference mentioned above.
- New 266 topological material candidates, not explored before.
- Re-predicted 68 topological material candidates found in recent literature.
-	The attention-based MPNN model achieved a very close result to ElemNet, probably hitting the limit of accuracies reachable with only the chemical stoichiometry.
-	Early stopping based on the performance over the validation set prevented overfitting and ensured generalization across both tasks.
-	The held-out test set performance closely matched validation performance, indicating that the models did not overfit and that the learned composition–topology relationships are robust and transferable.
-	Analysis of learned representations suggests that certain elemental groups and combinations exhibit strong correlations with topological phases, consistent with known chemical trends in SOC-driven band inversion and symmetry-protected states.
## Future Directions:
-	Extend to magnetic topological materials where DFT calculations suffer from unreliability more than weakly-correlated nonmagnetic materials discussed here.
-	Implementation of deeper Neural Networks of other architectures to test if the chemical composition by itself can lead to a more powerful model.
-	Decorate the chemical composition-only models develop here with more electronic and structural features to enhance its overall performance.
